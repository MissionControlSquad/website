AI agents are getting remarkably good at executing tasks autonomously. They can write code, research topics, draft content, and even coordinate with other agents. But autonomy without oversight is a recipe for trouble.

## The Problem with Fully Autonomous Agents

When you let AI agents run without guardrails, things can go wrong in ways that are hard to predict:

- **Hallucinated outputs** get shipped to production without anyone catching them
- **Scope creep** happens when agents interpret tasks too broadly
- **Quality drift** occurs gradually as small errors compound over time
- **Security risks** emerge when agents access systems without review

## What Human-in-the-Loop Actually Means

Human-in-the-loop isn't about micromanaging every action. It's about creating strategic checkpoints where humans can review, approve, or redirect agent work.

Think of it like a code review process. Developers don't write code and push directly to production (or they shouldn't). They create pull requests, get reviews, and merge after approval. The same principle applies to AI agent work.

## When to Use Human Oversight

Not every task needs the same level of oversight. Here's a simple framework:

- **High oversight**: Customer-facing content, code deployments, financial decisions
- **Medium oversight**: Internal reports, data analysis, research summaries
- **Low oversight**: Routine data processing, formatting, simple transformations

The key is matching the level of oversight to the risk of the task.
